Knowledge Lab / 01

Learn the Basics

Every physical quantity answers a question about the world. The key is knowing whether magnitude alone is enough, or whether direction is essential too.

01 / Scalar Quantities

How much?

A scalar quantity is fully described by its magnitude and an appropriate unit, where applicable. It does not require a direction.

Imagine a thermometer showing 22 °C. The measurement is complete without pointing anywhere.

MassTimeTemperatureDistanceSpeedEnergyPressureDensityVolumeElectric Charge
02 / Vector Quantities

How much, and which way?

A vector quantity requires both magnitude and direction to be fully specified.

Imagine walking 5 metres east. The direction is part of the information, not an optional extra.

DisplacementVelocityAccelerationForceWeightMomentumTorque

Quick Reference

Know the Difference

QuantityTypeWhy?
DistanceSCALARIt describes how much ground is covered, not a direction.
DisplacementVECTORIt describes a change in position with a direction.
SpeedSCALARIt tells how fast something moves.
VelocityVECTORIt combines speed with direction of motion.
MassSCALARIt measures inertia and has no direction.
WeightVECTORIt is a gravitational force, directed toward the attracting body.
EnergySCALARIt is measured by an amount, such as 50 joules.
ForceVECTORA force has magnitude and a direction of action.
TorqueVECTORIn three-dimensional classical mechanics, torque is an axial vector.
